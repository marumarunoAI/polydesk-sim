fair is the desk's own probability and mid is the market price, halfway between the best buy and sell orders. a maker leaves a limit order and waits, a taker hits an order that is already there and pays the fee. Kelly is the formula that sizes a bet from your edge, and pp is percentage points.

five strategies, each with a kill rule
1. Liquidation Magnet
a barrier market is an option. "will Bitcoin reach $X this month" pays if any Binance 1-minute candle touches the level once. traders call that a one-touch option, and any options textbook prices it.
`touch ≈ 2 × N(−|ln(X/S)| / (σ√T))`
S is the price now, X the level, σ how much the price swings, T the time left, N the bell-curve table. you do not do this math. the script returns one number, the fair chance of a touch.
the crowd prices these markets by how far the level looks. what actually breaks a barrier is a cascade of liquidations, and only the tape shows where they sit.
a study of 4,860 Polymarket Bitcoin and Ethereum contracts found the biggest gaps against options math in exactly these reach and dip markets: about 3.8 points when a market lists, close to zero by expiry. the edge is richest in the first days, so the desk prices every new barrier the morning it appears.
 
the #1 crypto wallet on Polymarket lives here and holds a position for about 100 hours. it bought "BTC dip to $77,500 in October" at 0.262 and made $56K. +$259K in the last 30 days.
https://polymarket.com/profile/0xf705fa045201391d9632b7f3cde06a5e24453ca7?via=argona
signal: `fair_touch` plus the base rate (how often Bitcoin moved this far in this many days). then the tape shows a liquidation cluster between price and the barrier, $10B or more. on November 11 the tape showed $12.1B to $14.1B stacked 3% above spot. that cluster comes from Minara's liquidation map, the one input no free API gives you.
entry: fair and market differ by more than fee + 2 pp, and the tape points the same way. limit order.
size: Kelly × 0.1 to 0.25, at most 3% of bankroll per barrier. all Bitcoin barriers count as one bet, 6% of bankroll in total.
exit: the gap shrinks under 0.5 pp, or 24 hours before expiry.
kill: after 30 resolved trades the desk's Brier score (how far your probabilities were from what happened) is no better than the market's.
 
realised volatility runs low around big events. before the Fed meeting on December 9 and 10, widen the range by hand or take implied volatility from Deribit, which is free.
2. The Longshot Tax
people overpay for lottery tickets. across 8,690 Polymarket markets, outcomes priced at 1 to 5 cents won 1.2% of the time against a price of 2.4%.
so the desk sells the lottery. it bets NO on the far levels, called strikes, inside "what price will Bitcoin hit in 2026", and only on days when the tape shows no cascade loading.
 
the #3 crypto wallet does exactly this. it keeps 83% of its value on NO and made $76K in 30 days.
the pattern holds across the board. a backtest of 80 closed crypto touch markets found YES priced above what actually happened in every probability band, and selling it 30 days out returned +3.3% a month. on reach markets that was 14 wins out of 14.
https://polymarket.com/profile/0xcc500cbcc8b7cf5bd21975ebbea34f21b5644c82?via=argona
signal: the market's YES price sits above `fair_touch` on a far strike, at least 2× the math. on the far wings it is often 6× to 25×.
veto: a liquidation cluster near the strike, funding at an extreme (5× the normal 0.01% per 8 hours or more), the Fed meeting inside the window, or locked tokens released before expiry on an altcoin market.
entry: a resting limit order on NO. never at 0.97 or above.
size: at most 3% of bankroll per barrier, because a loss takes the whole position.
exit: under 0.5 pp of edge, 24 hours before expiry, or the moment a veto appears.
kill: one loss bigger than three months of this strategy's gains.
 
every veto on that list is a line from the morning tape. no Minara, no vetoes, no trade.
3. Noon Print and the Mirror
"Bitcoin above $X on November 11" resolves on the Binance 1-minute close at 12:00 ET. the same day has a row of these markets at different levels, the ladder, and one textbook formula, `N(d2)`, prices all of them.
 
the Mirror is the second check. take the same level and window in a "hit" market and an "above" market. touching a level is about twice as likely as closing above it.
hit ≈ 2 × above
this is the reflection principle from options math: a price that touches a level is as likely to finish below it as above it, so the touch is worth about twice the close.
 
when the two prices sit far from that ratio, one market is off. the mismatch tells the Quant which market to recheck, and the tape tells it which side is wrong.
signal: the level priced furthest from the formula, or a hit/above pair priced below 1.6 or above 2.4.
the tape picks the side: a macro release before noon ET, funding and open interest, yesterday's ETF flow.
entry: gap above fee + 2 pp, limit order, sized like strategy 1.
exit: hold to the noon print, or leave under 0.5 pp.
kill: the same Brier gate as strategy 1. nothing shorter than daily, ever.
 
4. Premarket Peek
some markets ask what a new token will be worth, like "<token> FDV above $X one day after launch". FDV is the token price times its total supply.
the token often trades somewhere before launch. that premarket price times supply is an FDV the crowd mostly does not see.
 
premarket prices sit in no free API, so this whole strategy runs through Minara. if you skipped the link above, here it is again: https://minara.ai/r/ARGONA0X
signal: `implied_FDV = premarket price × total supply`, set against every strike.
haircut: 30%. Merlin opened 30% under its premarket, Wormhole sat 40% under it three days later, while Hyperliquid and Aevo premarkets usually converge on the launch price in the last 10 days.
entry: only strikes at least 25% away from implied FDV after the haircut. limit order.
size: at most 5 to 10% of visible depth, because these books are thin.
exit: at resolution. leave if the premarket goes quiet or the launch date moves.
kill: no premarket venue, or the launch slips past the market's deadline. the rule itself is exact: total supply × price at 4:00 PM ET the day after launch, from the most liquid source, and no launch by the deadline resolves NO.
